Design Phase

I prepared the initial team contract and created the machine learning code and analysis for the Airbnb business analysis project. The contract provided a starting point for organising the team's work. My analytical contribution connected the business question about pricing opportunities in New York City with a workflow for data preparation, segmentation, price prediction and comparison of price-demand patterns.

Room type and distance from the city centre were the only features used to form the baseline clusters. The original dataset had no demand column, so review activity was used to derive a demand proxy. Advertised price and this proxy were then used to compare the segments, rather than to determine cluster membership. K-prototypes accommodated the combination of categorical and numerical features (Huang, 1998). I compared candidate cluster counts using silhouette scores, stability, size and interpretability, drawing on Rousseeuw (1987), and included an extended feature set to assess whether availability and stay behaviour added useful information.

For pricing, I compared a median benchmark, OLS, Elastic Net, Random Forest and CatBoost using an 80/20 split and five-fold cross-validation on the training data. A logarithmic price target addressed the strongly skewed advertised rates. Property, location and host features reflected the pricing factors examined by Gibbs et al. (2018). Because booking data were unavailable, the demand analysis used a review-based proxy, with controls and sensitivity checks to examine how the findings depended on its assumptions. This structure linked model evaluation to an interpretable business question.

Implemented Solution

The implementation comprises five notebooks covering baseline and extended clustering, pricing models, segment pricing and market-wide robustness checks. The cleaned 2019 NYC dataset contained 31,340 listings, with 26,175 meeting the 30-day availability threshold used for the main demand analysis. Saved labels, predictions, model files and result tables made the analytical stages traceable.

Candidate solutions used repeated random initialisations, with stability assessed across runs to check sensitivity to starting conditions. The five-cluster baseline achieved a silhouette score of 0.660 and stability ARI of 0.763. The extended solution selected two clusters with a lower silhouette of 0.406 but higher stability of 0.883. The baseline was retained for its clearer separation and useful market detail, with silhouette interpretation considered alongside stability and interpretability (Rousseeuw, 1987). This comparison showed why additional features did not automatically produce more useful segments.

Model selection used five-fold cross-validation on the training data, reserving the held-out test set for evaluation. A median benchmark and linear models provided reference points for assessing the tree models, while error breakdowns by room type, borough and price band exposed uneven performance. CatBoost achieved the lowest cross-validation RMSE of 0.1902 on the log10 price scale, narrowly ahead of Random Forest. Its held-out dollar-scale MAE was $61.89. Room type and distance were the strongest predictors, consistent with the importance of property and location characteristics in Gibbs et al. (2018) and Deboosere et al. (2019). The segment analysis identified central entire homes as the strongest candidate for premium-price testing, less-central entire homes as having the highest review-based demand, and outer private rooms as particularly price-sensitive. Alternative demand caps and a stricter availability threshold preserved these main patterns.

The limitations concern interpretation and generalisation. Reviews measure activity rather than bookings or occupancy, and longer stays can generate fewer reviews at similar occupancy. Advertised prices exclude transaction-level adjustments, while the 2019 snapshot cannot establish current market conditions or causal price effects. Shared rooms were not recovered as a distinct segment. Prediction was also less reliable at the expensive end of the market, with top-quartile MAE around $163 and overall dollar-scale R-squared of 0.156. These findings support segment-specific pricing experiments, with updated booking and revenue data needed before recommending individual prices.
